## Changed on 2026-11-13, unproven until the run of 22 November

Live data read on 13 November showed three measurement defects and one serving defect. This change fixes them in code, proven offline by the tests named; nothing here has run live, and the first research run that carries it is the scheduled one on Sunday 22 November 2026. The packet's shape is unchanged (its schema is shared with Control Center and closed); only what `probes[].citations` and `we_cited` mean changed, as `docs/PACKET.md` now says.

- **ChatGPT searches on every probe.** `src/clients/openai.ts` sends `tool_choice: 'required'` (web search is its only tool) and asks for each search's sources; most ChatGPT probes since September had cited nothing because the model answered from memory. Its citations are the `url_citation` annotations in the order they sit in the text. Every probe now searches, so its deadline is four minutes, as Claude's is, not the 120 seconds that already timed some ChatGPT probes out on 8 November; and its spend cap estimate is 0.05 (was 0.03), because every probe now pays for a search and reads its content, and nothing meters OpenAI usage yet. Proven against faked responses in `tests/clients.test.ts`; unproven: that the live Responses API takes `tool_choice: 'required'` with the hosted tool for `gpt-5`, what share of answers then cite a source, what a forced-search probe really costs (until a provider bill for the 22 November run is read against 0.05, the cap may under-count ChatGPT), and how many ChatGPT probes still fail: read `probes_failed` for chatgpt in the 22 November run's job logs.
- **Claude's first citation is the first source its text cites.** It was the first raw search hit, because `src/clients/anthropic.ts` collected every URL in block order and the search results come before the text. Now only the `web_search_result_location` citations on text blocks count, across a paused search's turns; the search results are kept as what it retrieved. Claude runs at most two searches a probe (`CAPS.CLAUDE_API_SEARCH_MAX_USES`, was five), and its estimate is 0.07 (was 0.04). Unproven: the metered cost at two searches, and how often dynamic filtering searches from code (counted from the usage object when it does).
- **Perplexity's citations follow its `[n]` markers** in order of first appearance; the whole source list is what it retrieved.
- **The first citation is measured before cleaning.** The packet drops a URL carrying an email or an @handle, which could promote a later source to first. `src/pipeline/40-probe.ts` now measures each probe from its raw inline citations (`src/lib/probeDetail.ts`: first citation ours, our position, cited anywhere, searched, no citation) and the run summary counts `queries_cited_first` from that (`src/protocol/measure.ts`). The sealed summary also carries, per subject, questions cited anywhere and per engine its answers, searches and answers with no citation, and per job the web search requests Claude's responses reported; counts only. Proven in `tests/40-probe.test.ts` and `tests/protocol.test.ts`.
- **Control Center's share reads the packet, so it will not match this engine's count until its reader changes.** The North Star share Krish reads is computed in Control Center (`READERS['aeo-engine']` in its `api/_northStarRefresh.ts`) from the packets' probes, against its own domain lists; the engine's own `queries_cited_first` is counted here from the sealed summary. From 22 November the two read the same inline, ordered citations, but differ unless that reader also applies three rules this engine now does: a path-aware match (`urlMatchesDomain`: a domain with a path counts only under that path), Krish's ruled corrections in `config/own-domains.json` (fractionl.us never ours; krishraja.ai, krishraja.com and themindmaker.ai ours for every prospect and for Mindmake), and `we_cited` from the inline citations and text only. One difference cannot be closed through the closed packet: this engine takes the first citation before cleaning, and the packet carries the cleaned list, so where an answer's first source carries an email or an @handle, Control Center sees its second source as first. The ask is in `docs/CONTROL-CENTER-BRIEF.md`, "The first citation: what Control Center's reader must match".
- **A domain with a path counts only under it.** `urlMatchesDomain` in `src/lib/domains.ts`: a profile page on a social network no longer makes every page on that host ours, for `we_cited` and for the first citation alike. `we_cited` reads the text and the inline citations apart, so a domain in another URL's query string no longer counts. Proven in `tests/lib.test.ts`.
- **Krish's ruled domains** (Ruling, Krish, 2026-11-13): `config/own-domains.json` adds krishraja.ai, krishraja.com and themindmaker.ai for every prospect and for the Mindmake venture, and never counts fractionl.us, whatever Control Center lists (`src/config/ownDomains.ts`, checked by `check:config`, proven in `tests/ownDomains.test.ts`). The file is public, so only a venture's product slug may key it: `check:config` refuses any other key, and a prospect or an aspiration is never named there or matched by one. Unproven: which slug Control Center gives the Mindmake venture subject; the overlay matches its slug or its product slug, `mindmake`.
- **Requested questions are asked first, and within the subject's USD share never dropped.** A venture engine's requested questions are probed first, in request order, and the subject's probe limit rises to hold them all plus five of its own (never above 30); before, they sorted last and a request of more than twenty could never be served. The share can still run out: each research job gets an even share of `AEO_MAX_USD_PER_RUN`, and a subject with a 25-question request needs about 4 USD of its share at today's estimates (0.13 a question for three assistants, thirty questions, the proposal and the digest), so at the default 25 USD the cap stops the engine's own questions from seven subjects and requested ones from eight (3.35 USD buys the proposal, the digest and the 25 requested questions on every assistant). When it does, or a failed call or the 30-question limit leaves one unasked, the request stays open in Control Center for a later run, and the research report says so by product and count with an `answer_request_not_served` hold, never a question. The probes also keep back the digest's estimate (and the voice pass's, when Krish has approved a rule), which are charged after them; before, a subject asking thirty questions could spend its whole share on probes and its digest fall back every run. Proven through `runAll`, the whole subject run as the CLI composes it, in `tests/answers.test.ts`: 25 requested questions beside 30 of high prior demand, served at a full share, and unserved, reported and the digest still charged at a share too small for them.
- **The research report** says, per subject, on how many questions we were cited anywhere, and per assistant on how many asks it searched, and carries one line on the changed measurement while the report's week begins before 7 December 2026 (`src/protocol/reports.ts`). The line says the measurement changed after the 8 November run, the last one made by the earlier code, so it stays true on a run Krish starts by hand before 22 November.
- **The research job allows 120 minutes** (was 90). A subject with a venture request may probe thirty questions, about 39 minutes at the 26 minutes for twenty measured on the first live run, so an attempt, the five-minute wait and a retry come to about 83. That is a projection: after the 22 November run, read the subject jobs' durations and record them here.
- **Fireflies is retired** (Control Center's tool catalog lists it as a retired route): the research workflow no longer passes `FIREFLIES_API_KEY`, so every venture and prospect packet says `fireflies_unavailable` and no call is classified. A themes call that returns nothing usable still fails its subject, because the packet's `themes_status` has no honest value for that case.
- **A cap bug the price change exposed:** the probe stage checked each engine against the USD cap on its own and then charged them together, so the last charge of a question could cross the cap and fail the subject. It now counts the question's engines together (`tests/40-probe.test.ts`).

## Known limits

- The demand score has no prompt-volume corpus behind it; every row's labels say so. Google volume enters only through Control Center's striking-distance rows.
- Recommendations for an aspiration mean "questions where the aspiration is absent"; the useful output for that kind is the playbook.
- `probes_skipped_cap` counts pairs skipped by the USD cap only. Queries beyond the per-subject probe limit (20, or the requested questions plus five, never above 30) simply carry no probes. A venture asking more than 30 questions of one subject in one run would have the rest unasked, and its request unserved until a later run asks them.
- Search behaviour (whether an engine searched, its queries, what it retrieved) is kept for counts only: the packet's shape is closed, so Control Center sees only the cleaned inline citations and `we_cited`.
- `queries_cited_first` uses "at least one assistant's first citation is ours" as its rule. That rule is the engine's reading of the objective, not a ruling; a stricter one (every assistant, or a majority) is a one-line change in `src/protocol/measure.ts`.
- A second full research run on the same UTC day gets a 409 on its protocol report, because one full run a day has one `run_id`. The attempt fails with `report_conflict`; the packets still land.
- An instruction from Krish lapses 7 days after he gives it, and the research runs fortnightly. The daily run never starts paid research, so an instruction given in an off week is acted on only if he starts a run by hand before it lapses; the daily run's `blocked` ack says so, with the dates.
- The calls window is the last fourteen days, the fortnight between scheduled runs. A run started by hand between them reads calls the next scheduled run reads again; a scheduled run that GitHub starts earlier than the last one leaves a gap of at most those hours.
